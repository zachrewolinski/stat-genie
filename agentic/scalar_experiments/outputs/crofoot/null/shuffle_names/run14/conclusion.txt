Using 58 recorded intergroup contests, I fit a logistic regression model with focal-group victory (m_focal) as the outcome and two standardized predictors: relative group size (log ratio of focal to other group size) and contest location (how much closer the focal group is to its own home-range center than the opponent is to theirs). The overall focal-group win rate is 0.55. For relative group size, the standardized coefficient is -0.31 with p-value 0.292, corresponding to an odds ratio of 0.73 for a one-standard-deviation increase; focal groups that are relatively larger (above the median log size ratio) win about 0.51 of contests versus 0.61 when they are relatively smaller. For contest location, the standardized coefficient is 0.19 with p-value 0.527, and an odds ratio of 1.20 per one-standard-deviation advantage in being closer to the center of their own home range; contests where the focal group has a stronger location advantage (above-median location score) have a win rate of 0.48 compared to 0.62 when their location advantage is weaker. Combining the size and location effects, the strength-of-evidence score for an influence of relative group size and contest location on victory probability is mapped to 7 on a 0–100 Likert scale, so the data support a 'No' answer: there is limited evidence that both relative group size and contest location meaningfully affect the probability that a focal capuchin group wins an intergroup contest."}
