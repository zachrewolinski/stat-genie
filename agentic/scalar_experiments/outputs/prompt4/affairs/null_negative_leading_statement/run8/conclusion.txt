The share reporting any affair was 25.7% without children versus 24.7% with children, and the mean affair score was 1.69 versus 1.36, so any reduction among parents is very small. A logistic regression of any affair on an indicator for having children produced a small negative coefficient (about -0.06 on the log-odds scale, odds ratio roughly 0.94) with a high p value around 0.78; adding controls for age, years married, religiousness, education, occupation, marital satisfaction rating, and gender yielded a similarly small and non significant coefficient (p about 0.87) and predicted probabilities of any affair of about 0.249 for respondents without children and 0.243 for those with children. These results indicate that the presence of children is at most associated with a negligible reduction in extramarital affairs, and the uncertainty bands comfortably include both modest decreases and modest increases, so the data do not provide strong evidence that having children meaningfully decreases engagement in extramarital affairs."}
