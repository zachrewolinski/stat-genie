Across 94 storms, the femininity index has essentially no correlation with fatalities (r≈0.06 raw; r≈-0.04 with log fatalities). A Mann–Whitney test comparing male vs female names finds no difference in fatalities (p≈0.72). In OLS models of log fatalities controlling for storm severity (category, max wind, min pressure), the femininity index is not significant and is slightly negative (coef≈-0.022, p≈0.67); the female-name indicator is also not significant (coef≈-0.48, p≈0.25). Thus the data do not support the claim that more feminine names lead to fewer precautionary measures (which would imply higher fatalities), and the direction is weakly opposite. Given the lack of statistical significance and the proxy nature of fatalities for precautionary behavior, the evidence favors a “No.”"}
