The simple correlation between masfem and total deaths is small and positive (r≈0.12). In OLS models of log(deaths+1), the masfem coefficient is positive and not statistically significant in a bivariate model (β≈0.037, p≈0.454) and remains positive and non‑significant when controlling for storm intensity and context (wind, minimum pressure, category, damage, year; β≈0.020, p≈0.606; N=94). A sensitivity model without year yields the same non‑significant, positive result (p≈0.449). Thus, the data do not support fewer deaths for more feminine names; if anything, the association points in the opposite direction and is statistically indistinguishable from zero. Therefore, the claim that feminine names lead to fewer precautionary measures (as inferred via fatalities) is not supported here."}
