{"response":55,"explanation":"Research question: Does having children decrease engagement in extramarital affairs?\n\nThe dataset contains 601 married individuals from the Fair (1978) affairs study. Overall, 25.0% of respondents reported at least one extramarital affair in the past year.\n\nDescriptively, the proportion reporting any affair was 24.7% among respondents with children and 25.7% among those without children. This raw comparison provides an initial view of how affairs differ by parental status.\n\nTo account for other factors that might be related to affairs and to having children, I fit a logistic regression model predicting whether a respondent had any extramarital affair (binary outcome) from the presence of children, controlling for age, years married, religiousness, education, occupation, self-rated marital happiness, and gender. In this model, having children corresponds to an odds ratio of approximately 0.97 (p = 0.870) for engaging in an affair, compared with not having children. This indicates lower odds of affairs for parents relative to non-parents, after adjusting for the listed covariates.\n\nHowever, the effect of having children on the odds of an affair is not statistically significant at the conventional 5% level. Although the point estimate suggests lower odds for parents, the uncertainty is large enough that we cannot rule out no meaningful difference. Consequently, there is insufficient statistical evidence to claim that having children decreases engagement in extramarital affairs, and the response on the 0–100 scale is kept close to the middle to reflect this weak evidence."}
