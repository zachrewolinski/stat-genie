The femininity index (1–11 scale; column ‘category’) shows essentially zero bivariate association with deaths (Spearman rho=0.0017, p=0.987). In OLS models of log(deaths+1) controlling for storm intensity (max wind speed, minimum pressure, Saffir–Simpson category) and year, the femininity index is not significant (coef=0.024, p=0.574), and the binary female name indicator is also not significant (coef=0.221, p=0.448). A negative binomial model to account for over-dispersion (alpha≈1.8) yields only marginal evidence for the femininity index (p=0.068) and still non‑significance for the binary indicator (p=0.161). Because the effect is not consistent across robust specifications and does not reach conventional significance, the data do not support the claim that more feminine names lead to fewer precautionary measures (as proxied by higher deaths)."}
