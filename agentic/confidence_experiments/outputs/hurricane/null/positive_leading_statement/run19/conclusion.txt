Name femininity (masfem) shows essentially no association with deaths: Pearson r with log(deaths+1) = -0.044 and Spearman rho with deaths = -0.049 (p=0.64). In OLS models, masfem is not significant either unadjusted (coef = -0.021, p=0.66) or after controlling for storm severity and time (category, wind, minimum pressure, year; coef = -0.027, p=0.61). An interaction between femininity and category is also nonsignificant (p=0.32). Coefficients are negative and the confidence intervals include zero, so there is no evidence that more feminine names lead to higher fatalities (the predicted pattern if weaker precautionary responses were driving deaths). Given the lack of statistical support and the proxy nature of fatalities for precautionary measures, the data do not support the stated claim."}
