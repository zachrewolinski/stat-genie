# Does Having Children Decrease Engagement in Extramarital Affairs?

## Summary

After controlling for key confounders, we find **no statistically significant association** between having children and engagement in extramarital affairs. While a raw comparison suggests those with children report more affairs (mean 1.672 vs 0.912 occasions per year), this crude association reverses after adjusting for years married, religiousness, and marriage satisfaction. In the multivariable model, the children coefficient is -0.143 (95% CI: -0.830 to 0.545, p = 0.684), indicating neither a protective nor risk effect can be confidently established from these data.

## Main Result

**Model A (Unadjusted):** A simple comparison shows those with children report 0.760 more affair occasions (p = 0.011), suggesting children are associated with increased affairs.

**Model B (Adjusted):** After controlling for age, years married, gender, religiousness, education, occupation, and marriage rating, the association disappears. The adjusted coefficient for children is -0.143 (95% CI: -0.830 to 0.545, p = 0.684). The strongest predictors of affairs are:
- **Years married** (+0.169, p < 0.001): longer marriages associated with more affairs
- **Religiousness** (-0.478, p < 0.001): higher religiousness associated with fewer affairs
- **Marriage rating** (-0.712, p < 0.001): happier marriages associated with fewer affairs

## Robustness

The null finding holds under alternative specifications. A log-transformed outcome model yields a children coefficient of 0.014 (p = 0.872). The crude association likely reflects confounding: those with children tend to be married longer, which independently predicts affairs. Once duration and marriage quality are controlled, the children effect vanishes.

## Limitations

This is an observational study from a 1969 magazine survey with potential self-selection bias and social desirability effects. Causation cannot be inferred. The outcome measure is retrospective self-report and may be unreliable. The non-random sample limits generalizability. Unmeasured confounders (relationship dynamics, opportunity for affairs) may remain.
